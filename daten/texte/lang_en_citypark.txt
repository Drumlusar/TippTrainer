Early in the morning, the city park feels almost like a different place. The paths are still damp with dew, a thin mist lies over the pond, and only a few footsteps can be heard on the gravel. An older gentleman is walking his dog, a runner is doing her laps, and someone sits on a bench with a cup of coffee, watching the ducks.

As the morning goes on, the picture changes. Parents push strollers to the playground, school classes pass by in long rows, and the first visitors spread out their blankets on the large lawn. Gardeners are at work, trimming hedges, raking leaves, and watering the flower beds that were replanted in the spring. Without their work, the park would be hard to recognize after just a few weeks.

Around lunchtime, it gets crowded. Employees from the nearby offices use their break for a walk or eat the sandwiches they brought in the shade of the old trees. Some make phone calls, others read, and still others simply close their eyes for a few minutes. For many people, the park is a place where they can take a deep breath before the afternoon continues.

But a park is not only a place for people to relax. It also offers a home to many animals that would hardly find room in the densely built city. Birds nest in the hedges, hedgehogs and mice live under the roots of the trees, and on warm evenings bats flutter over the water. Insects find food in the flower meadows, especially when not every area is mowed regularly.

Green spaces also play an important role in the climate of a city. On hot summer days, it is noticeably cooler in the park than on the paved streets around it. The trees provide shade and release moisture through their leaves, the meadows store rainwater, and the ground heats up less. For this reason, many cities now make an effort to preserve existing parks and to create new green spaces.

In autumn, the park changes its colors once more. The leaves of the maples and chestnuts turn yellow, orange, and red, and children collect chestnuts in their pockets. The paths are covered with a soft layer of leaves that rustles with every step, and on clear days the low sun makes the whole park glow.

In the evening, the park slowly empties again. The last families pack up their things, teenagers meet on the steps by the pond, and the street lamps come on. When it gets dark, a special calm settles in. Only the wind in the leaves and the occasional call of an owl can still be heard, until everything begins again the next morning.
